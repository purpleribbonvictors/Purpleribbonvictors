Understanding epilepsy

Plain facts, no jargon, because real support starts with understanding. Save this page, share it with someone. You never know when it might help you make a life saving difference.

The basics

So, what is epilepsy?

Epilepsy is a neurological condition that causes recurring seizures. A seizure happens when there's a sudden burst of unusual electrical activity in the brain, and it can affect how a person moves, feels, or responds for a short time.

It's one of the most common neurological conditions in the world, and it can affect anyone at any age. Epilepsy isn't contagious, and it isn't caused by curses, witchcraft, or anything a person did wrong. In most cases, doctors can trace a cause such as a head injury, an infection, genetics, or another brain condition, though sometimes the cause is never fully known.

With the right diagnosis and treatment, many people with epilepsy live full, seizure free lives. That's really the whole point of this page: to replace fear with facts, so people can get on with living.

A quick note. This page is here to help you understand epilepsy in everyday terms. It isn't a replacement for medical advice. If you or someone you know is dealing with seizures, please see a doctor or neurologist for a proper diagnosis and care plan.
Myth or fact

The stories worth retiring

During a seizure, a person's jaw may tighten, they might bite their tongue, or their breathing may sound different. To someone seeing it for the first time, that can be frightening. Fear creates assumptions, and assumptions become myths.

Myth

People swallow their tongue during a seizure.

Fact

They don't, and physically, they can't. It's impossible for someone to swallow their tongue during a seizure. The tongue might relax or move differently because of muscle contractions, but it cannot disappear into the throat.

"The real problem isn't epilepsy. It's misinformation. Every time we replace a myth with the truth, we make our communities a little safer and a lot more compassionate."

If you're ever in this situation

Seizure first aid, in plain terms

Would you know what to do if someone near you had a seizure? Your actions can genuinely make a life saving difference. Here's what to remember.

Do

  • ✓ Stay calm.
  • ✓ Gently move dangerous or harmful objects out of the way.
  • ✓ Cushion their head with something soft, if you can.
  • ✓ Turn them gently onto their side once the seizure ends, if it's safe to.
  • ✓ Time the seizure.
  • ✓ Stay with them until they regain awareness.

Don't

  • ✕ Don't hold the person down.
  • ✕ Don't put anything in their mouth, not a spoon, not your fingers.
  • ✕ Don't try to hold their tongue.
  • ✕ Don't give food, water, or medication during the seizure.
  • ✕ Don't panic.

Call emergency services if

  • The seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes.
  • Another seizure starts right after the first one ends.
  • They're having trouble breathing afterward.
  • They've been injured.
  • It's their first seizure.

After the seizure

Stay with them until they're fully alert again. Offer reassurance, they might feel confused, tired, or embarrassed, and none of that needs fixing right away. Just give them time and space to rest.

Sometimes the greatest support you can offer is simply understanding what's happening and staying steady through it.
